MEXC lists tokens earlier than most large exchanges, which is the platform's main draw and the source of its main hazard. Early listings are thinly traded, and some of them are designed to extract money from buyers rather than build anything.

Most rug pulls are not invisible in advance. They leave structural traces that can be checked in a few minutes, and knowing what to look for removes most of the risk.

## Start with who holds the supply

The single most informative number is holder concentration. If a handful of wallets control most of the supply, those wallets can sell into any liquidity that exists, and nothing prevents them.

On MEXC listings, check the distribution before anything else. A token where the top wallets hold a dominant share is not an investment — it is a pending exit.

- A small number of wallets holding most of the supply is the clearest warning sign.
- Distribution that improves over time is healthier than distribution that does not.
- A single wallet holding a large share is a risk regardless of how the project describes itself.

## Check the liquidity, not the market cap

Market capitalisation on a micro-cap is close to meaningless. It is calculated from the last traded price, and on a thin book that price can be moved by a small order.

What matters is how much liquidity actually sits in the pool or the order book. A token with a large headline valuation and shallow liquidity cannot absorb selling, and the first meaningful exit collapses the price.

Compare the depth against the position size you intend to take. If your order would move the price materially, the market is thinner than it appears.

## Look for the unlock schedule

Locked tokens are future supply. If a large portion of the supply unlocks in the near future, that supply arrives as sell pressure regardless of how the chart looks today.

Projects publish vesting schedules in their documentation, and the information is usually there for anyone who reads it. What you are looking for is how much unlocks in the next few months and who receives it.

## Patterns that precede a rug

A few recurring shapes are worth recognising.

Liquidity that is removed shortly after launch is the classic pattern. A pool funded at listing and drained within days leaves holders with a token they cannot sell.

Team tokens unlocked earlier than the published schedule is another. So is a sudden change to the token contract, which can introduce functions that were not in the original.

A social presence that is entirely promotional, with no technical discussion and no critical voices, is a softer signal but a consistent one.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a token on a major exchange still rug pull?

Yes. A listing on any exchange, including MEXC, is not a guarantee of legitimacy. Exchanges list tokens; they do not audit the teams behind them.

### What is the fastest check before buying a micro-cap?

Holder concentration and liquidity depth. Those two numbers rule out most of the worst outcomes in under a minute.

### Does the referral discount protect me from a bad token?

No. It reduces your trading fee. It has no effect on the risk that the token itself collapses.
