## Why exchanges delist tokens

Exchanges remove trading pairs for several reasons: a project fails to meet ongoing listing standards, trading volume drops below a threshold, the team behind the token becomes unresponsive, or regulatory pressure forces removal. Gate.io is not unique in this — every exchange delists tokens — but the platform's large selection means the absolute number of delistings is higher than on exchanges with fewer pairs.

Delisting does not necessarily mean the token is worthless. But it often means the liquidity that made it tradable has dried up, and the easiest path to sell it has been removed.

## Signs a token may be delisted

Gate.io typically announces delistings in advance, giving users a window to withdraw or trade their holdings. But there are earlier warning signs you can watch for.

- Volume collapse: if a token's daily trading volume has been declining steadily for weeks, the exchange may remove it for failing to meet minimum thresholds.
- Withdrawal suspensions: if Gate.io temporarily suspends withdrawals for a specific token, this can precede a full delisting.
- Team silence: if the project's social channels go quiet or the team stops responding, the exchange may act to protect users.
- Regulatory news: if a token faces legal action or regulatory scrutiny in a major jurisdiction, exchanges often delist preemptively.

## What happens after delisting

Once a token is removed from Gate.io's trading pairs, you generally cannot buy or sell it on the platform. You may still be able to withdraw it to an external wallet, but you will need another venue to trade it — possibly a decentralized exchange, possibly nowhere at all.

The worst case is a token that is both delisted and has no other trading venue. In that scenario, your holdings become illiquid and effectively untradeable. This is not a hypothetical; it has happened to tokens on every major exchange.

## How to protect yourself

- Diversify across exchanges: do not hold all your altcoins on one platform.
- Set volume thresholds: if a token's daily volume drops below a level you consider safe, reduce your position.
- Monitor announcements: Gate.io publishes delisting notices. Check the announcements page regularly if you hold smaller-cap tokens.
- Avoid the deepest micro-caps: tokens with the smallest market caps and thinnest order books carry the highest delisting risk.

Gate.io's 1,700+ pair selection is a double-edged sword — it gives you access to tokens others do not carry, but it also means more tokens are at risk of removal.

## Risks to keep in mind

- Timing risk: delisting announcements may give only a short window to act.
- Withdrawal limits: after delisting, Gate.io may impose withdrawal limits on the affected token.
- Tax implications: converting a delisted token to another asset may trigger a taxable event depending on your jurisdiction.
- Emotional attachment: holders sometimes refuse to sell a losing token because they do not want to realise the loss, right up until delisting removes the choice.
