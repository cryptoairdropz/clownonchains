OKX Web3 and MetaMask both give you self-custody wallet access to decentralized applications, but they take different approaches to the same problem. OKX Web3 is built by a major exchange and emphasizes integration with a broader ecosystem. MetaMask is the standalone pioneer that most DeFi users started with.

## Self-custody means you hold the keys

In both wallets, you control your own seed phrase. That phrase is the only way to recover your funds if you lose access to the device. Neither OKX Web3 nor MetaMask can reset it for you. The moment you write down your seed phrase, you are your own bank — for better and for worse.

## How OKX Web3 differs from MetaMask

OKX Web3 is developed by OKX, one of the largest centralized exchanges in the world. That backing means the wallet is maintained by a known entity with a reputation to protect. The wallet integrates with the broader OKX ecosystem, so you can move between centralized and decentralized features more easily. MetaMask, by contrast, is developed by Consensum and has no exchange parent. It is the most widely used standalone DeFi wallet, which means broader dapp support and a larger user base.

- OKX Web3: built by a major exchange, self-custody, multichain support
- MetaMask: standalone, widely adopted, browser extension and mobile app
- Both: seed phrase backup, dapp browser, token swaps

## Risks with any self-custody wallet

Self-custody is a feature and a risk at the same time. If you lose your seed phrase, your funds are gone. If someone else gets it, they can drain your wallet before you notice. Phishing sites that mimic wallet login pages are common. Smart contract risk also applies: when you interact with a dapp, you are trusting that contract's code, not just the wallet interface.

## When each wallet makes more sense

OKX Web3 makes sense if you already use the OKX exchange and want a bridge between centralized and decentralized finance in one app. MetaMask makes sense if you want the widest possible dapp compatibility and a wallet that has been tested across years of DeFi activity. Both are reasonable choices — the better question is whether you are ready for the responsibility that comes with self-custody.


## Seed phrase security compared

Both wallets use the same 12-word seed phrase standard, which means a phrase generated in one wallet can be imported into the other. This portability is convenient but also a risk: if you enter your seed phrase into a phishing site pretending to be either wallet, the attacker gains access to all your funds. OKX Web3 benefits from being part of a larger security infrastructure, but the seed phrase itself is only as secure as the device where it is stored. MetaMask has faced similar phishing challenges over the years. In both cases, hardware wallet integration provides the strongest protection for significant holdings.

## Mobile experience compared

Both wallets offer mobile apps, but the experience differs. OKX Web3 is designed as a multichain wallet from the ground up, with a mobile interface that makes switching between chains straightforward. MetaMask's mobile app has improved significantly but still feels like a companion to the browser extension rather than a standalone product. If you primarily trade on mobile, OKX Web3 offers a more integrated experience. If you split time between desktop and mobile, MetaMask's browser extension remains the more seamless option for interacting with dapps on desktop.
## Frequently asked questions

### Is OKX Web3 safer than MetaMask?

For holding funds, both are self-custody, so security depends on how you protect your seed phrase. OKX Web3 benefits from being built by a major exchange, but that also means it is a larger target for phishing attacks.

### Can I use the same seed phrase on both wallets?

Yes. Both OKX Web3 and MetaMask use the same seed phrase standard, so you can import a MetaMask wallet into OKX Web3 and vice versa.

### Does the CLOWNZ code apply to all OKX Web3 features?

The code applies a fee discount to eligible transactions within the OKX Web3 ecosystem. Verify the specific terms on the referral page before assuming it applies to every action.
