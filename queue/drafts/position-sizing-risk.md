Position sizing is the single most reliable risk control in trading, yet most retail traders treat it as an afterthought. They spend hours analyzing entry points and chart patterns while leaving position size to guesswork. This article explains why position sizing matters more than timing, how to calculate it properly, and what methods actually work in practice.

## Why position size matters more than entry timing

A trader with a mediocre entry strategy but disciplined position sizing will outperform a trader with excellent entries and random sizing over any meaningful sample size. The reason is straightforward: position size determines how much you lose when you are wrong. If you risk 10% of your account on a single trade, ten consecutive losses wipe you out. If you risk 1%, you can survive long enough for your edge to play out.

The math is unforgiving. Losing 50% of your account requires a 100% gain just to break even. Losing 90% requires a 900% gain. Position sizing is the tool that keeps you in the game long enough for your strategy to work. Without it, even a profitable system can destroy an account during a normal drawdown period.

## Fixed fractional sizing

The most common approach is fixed fractional sizing, where you risk a set percentage of your current account balance on each trade. Many professional traders use between 0.5% and 2% per trade. The exact number depends on your win rate, your average win-to-loss ratio, and your psychological tolerance for drawdowns.

- Risk 0.5% if you are new or have a low win rate
- Risk 1% if you have a proven edge and stable emotions
- Risk 2% only if you have a high win rate and can handle volatility

The key is consistency. Changing your risk percentage based on recent results or emotions defeats the purpose entirely. A 1% risk that becomes 3% after a loss and 0.5% after a win is no longer a system. It is gambling with extra steps.

## The Kelly criterion and its limits

The Kelly criterion is a mathematical formula that calculates the optimal bet size based on your win rate and payoff ratio. In theory, it maximizes long-term growth. In practice, it often suggests bet sizes that are too aggressive for real-world conditions.

Most traders who use Kelly apply a fractional version, such as half-Kelly or quarter-Kelly, to reduce volatility and account for estimation errors in their win rate and payoff assumptions. Even then, the formula requires accurate inputs that are difficult to estimate from limited trading history. A small error in your win rate estimate can lead to dramatically oversized positions.

## Position sizing in crypto specifically

Crypto markets amplify position sizing challenges through extreme volatility, 24/7 trading, and sudden liquidity gaps. A position that would be reasonable in equities might be reckless in a low-cap altcoin that can move 30% in an hour.

Several adjustments make sense for crypto:

- Reduce position sizes for assets with lower liquidity
- Account for funding rates when holding leveraged positions overnight
- Consider correlation between assets when sizing a portfolio
- Use wider stop-losses but smaller positions to accommodate volatility
- Factor in exchange risk when holding funds on trading platforms

## Common mistakes and how to avoid them

The most frequent position sizing errors are increasing size after losses to recover quickly, decreasing size after wins out of fear, and using the same dollar amount regardless of account size. All three break the mathematical foundation that makes position sizing effective.

Another mistake is confusing position size with leverage. A small position with high leverage can still result in significant losses if the market moves against you. Leverage multiplies both gains and losses, so it must be factored into your total risk calculation. A 1% position with 10x leverage is effectively a 10% risk.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good position size for beginners?

Beginners should risk no more than 0.5% to 1% of their account per trade. This allows room for error while learning and prevents catastrophic losses during the inevitable losing streaks that every trader experiences.

### How do I calculate position size?

Divide your account risk (account size multiplied by risk percentage) by the distance between your entry and stop-loss. The result is the number of units you can buy. Most trading platforms and spreadsheet tools can automate this calculation.

### Does position sizing guarantee profits?

No. Position sizing controls losses and preserves capital, but it cannot create an edge where none exists. You still need a strategy with positive expectancy. Position sizing ensures you survive long enough for that expectancy to materialize.
