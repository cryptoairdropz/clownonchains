Cove markets itself as a Solana and EVM copy-trading bot that runs entirely in Telegram. After examining its features, fee structure, and risk profile, this assessment covers what Cove does well, where it falls short, and who it suits best.

## What Cove does well

Cove's main strength is its focused feature set. Rather than trying to be everything, it concentrates on copy trading — automatically mirroring another wallet's trades — and does it across both Solana and EVM chains. That cross-chain support is useful if you want to follow wallets trading on different networks without switching tools.

The Telegram interface is compact and navigable. You can track your PnL, see open positions, and manage your copied wallets without leaving the chat. For traders who live in Telegram, that integration is genuine convenience.

The setup process is quick. You create or import a wallet, deposit SOL, pick a wallet to copy, and allocate funds. The whole process takes a few minutes and does not require any technical knowledge of on-chain mechanics.

## Where Cove falls short

Cove charges around 1% per trade. This is in line with comparable Telegram bots but significantly higher than a centralised exchange. For frequent traders, the fee difference adds up quickly — a hundred trades in a month means a hundred 1% deductions from your principal before any profit.

The copy-trading feature, while convenient, has structural limitations. You are executing after the wallet you follow, which means you get a worse entry price. On volatile tokens, that gap between their entry and yours can be the difference between profit and loss.

Cove does not offer the broader toolset that some competitors provide. There is no built-in sniper for new token launches, no bridge for moving assets between chains, and no advanced order types. If you need those features, Cove will feel limiting.

## The custody problem

This applies to all Telegram bots, not just Cove, but it bears repeating. Your funds sit in the bot's wallet, not in a wallet where you control the seed phrase. Cove does not give you independent custody of your deposited assets.

If Cove's infrastructure is compromised, your balance is exposed. There is no insurance fund, no proof-of-reserves publication, and no recovery mechanism if the bot is hacked. You are trusting the operators entirely.

This is not a reason to avoid Cove, but it is a reason to keep your bot wallet balance limited. Never deposit more than you are prepared to lose.

## Risks specific to copy trading

Copy trading introduces risks that are separate from custody:

- A wallet that was profitable yesterday may not be profitable tomorrow
- Slippage is heavy on thin tokens and can erode copied gains
- You have no control over the strategy you are copying — the wallet can change direction at any time
- New tokens, which are common copy-trading targets, carry the highest risk in crypto

The wallet you copy has no obligation to you. It can make reckless trades, chase scams, or rug pull its own followers. You inherit all of that risk by enabling copy trading.

## Who Cove suits best

Cove works for traders who want a simple, Telegram-native copy-trading experience across Solana and EVM chains. If you value ease of use and do not need a sniper or bridge, the focused feature set is clean and functional.

It does not suit traders who want advanced tools, low fees, or self-custody. For those needs, a centralised exchange or a non-custodial wallet with DEX access may be a better fit.

Cove is a reasonable choice for its specific niche. It does copy trading well, it works across the chains that matter most, and its interface is clean. The question is whether that niche matches what you actually need from a trading tool.
