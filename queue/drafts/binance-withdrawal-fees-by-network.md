Every crypto withdrawal from Binance charges a network fee, and the amount varies dramatically depending on which blockchain you choose. Sending Bitcoin on the Bitcoin network costs more than sending it on the BNB Smart Chain. Understanding these differences can save you real money, especially if you withdraw frequently. This article explains how Binance sets withdrawal fees by network and how to pick the cheapest option.

## Why withdrawal fees differ by network

When you withdraw crypto from Binance, you choose a destination network. Bitcoin can be sent on the Bitcoin mainnet, the Lightning Network, or the BNB Smart Chain as a wrapped token. Each network has different congestion levels and fee structures. Binance passes the actual network cost to the user, plus a small exchange fee. The Bitcoin mainnet typically charges the highest withdrawal fees because block space is limited and demand is high. During periods of congestion, a single Bitcoin withdrawal can cost several dollars. By contrast, sending the same value as a BEP-20 token on BNB Smart Chain costs a fraction of a cent.

## How to check withdrawal fees before confirming

Binance displays the exact withdrawal fee on the confirmation screen before you finalise the transaction. The fee is shown in the coin being withdrawn, not in a stablecoin. To compare networks, go to the withdrawal page for the coin you want to send, select the network dropdown to see available options, note the fee listed next to each network, and compare the fee to the amount you are withdrawing. If you are withdrawing $50 worth of Bitcoin and the mainnet fee is $2, that is 4% of your withdrawal. Switching to a lower-fee network, if the receiving wallet supports it, reduces that cost to under $1.

## The trade-off: speed, compatibility, and risk

Choosing a cheaper network is not always straightforward. The receiving wallet or exchange must support the same network. Sending Bitcoin via the Lightning Network to a wallet that only accepts on-chain Bitcoin will result in lost funds. Low-fee networks like BNB Smart Chain are fast and cheap but not all wallets support BEP-20 tokens. Bitcoin mainnet is universally compatible but expensive during congestion. Lightning Network offers near-instant, low-fee Bitcoin transfers but requires a compatible wallet. Ethereum mainnet is widely supported but gas fees fluctuate with network demand. Always verify the receiving address supports the selected network before confirming.

## Tips to minimise total withdrawal costs

- Batch withdrawals: consolidate multiple small balances into one transaction
- Time your withdrawals: Bitcoin fees drop during weekends and low-activity periods
- Use the right network: if the receiver supports multiple chains, pick the cheapest
- Consider Binance Convert: swapping to a low-fee coin before withdrawing can reduce costs
- Check for zero-fee promotions: Binance occasionally waives withdrawal fees for specific coins

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I choose any network for any coin?

No. Each coin on Binance supports a specific set of networks. The available options appear in the dropdown on the withdrawal page. You can only select from the listed networks.

### What happens if I send to the wrong network?

If the receiving wallet does not support the network you selected, the funds may be lost permanently. Always double-check the network before confirming a withdrawal.

### Does the 20% referral discount apply to withdrawal fees?

No. The 20% fee discount applies to trading fees only. Withdrawal fees are determined by the blockchain network and are not affected by referral status.
