OKX fee tiers determine how much you pay per trade, but the structure confuses many users. The platform uses a 30-day volume model that shifts traders between bands, and your effective rate depends on whether your orders add or remove liquidity. This article explains how the bands work, where the maker-taker split matters, and how the referral discount changes the math.

## How the 30-day volume model works

OKX calculates your tier based on trading volume over the past 30 days. The measurement rolls forward daily, so a single high-volume day can lift your tier, while a quiet month can drop you back down. The base taker rate is 0.10% and the base maker rate is 0.08%, which apply to accounts at the lowest volume band.

Higher volume unlocks lower rates. The exact thresholds and the specific rates at each band can change, so the only reliable source is the current fee schedule published on OKX's website. What does not change is the principle: the more you trade, the less you pay per order. For casual traders buying and selling small amounts, the base rates are what you will actually experience.

## Why the maker-taker split matters

A maker order adds liquidity to the order book — a limit order that does not fill immediately. A taker order removes liquidity — a market order or a limit order that fills against an existing order. OKX charges less for makers (0.08%) than takers (0.10%) because makers provide the liquidity that makes the market function.

If you place limit orders that rest on the book, you pay the lower rate. If you cross the spread with market orders, you pay the higher rate. The 0.02% difference seems small, but it compounds across hundreds of trades. A trader who consistently uses limit orders saves the equivalent of one full trade fee for every ten they place.

## How the referral discount applies

The OKX referral code ENJOYDISCOUNT gives new accounts a 20% fee discount. This discount applies on top of whatever tier your volume qualifies you for. If you are at the base tier, the discount reduces the taker rate by a fifth of 0.10% and the maker rate by a fifth of 0.08%. If you reach a higher tier, the discount stacks with the already-lower band rate.

The code fills in automatically when you sign up through the referral link. There is no separate activation step, and the discount applies from your first trade. Combine the referral discount with maker-order discipline — placing limit orders rather than market orders — to minimize your effective rate.

## Risks and things that go wrong

Fee tiers can create perverse incentives. Some traders inflate their volume to reach a lower band, paying more in absolute fees just to save on a percentage. Calculate whether the extra volume actually saves money before chasing a tier. The rolling 30-day window also means your tier can drop without warning if you stop trading actively.

Another trap is confusing the discount with a permanent rate lock. The ENJOYDISCOUNT code applies a 20% discount, but the underlying tier structure still shifts with your volume. If your volume drops, your base rate rises even though the discount remains. Always check the current fee page before assuming your rate has not changed.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I check my current fee tier on OKX?

OKX displays your tier and applicable rates in the fee schedule section of your account dashboard. The page shows both maker and taker rates for your current volume band and updates as your 30-day volume changes.

### Does the ENJOYDISCOUNT code expire?

The code is active and applies a fee discount to new accounts. The discount itself does not have a published expiry date, but OKX can modify referral terms at any time, so verify the current status before relying on it long term.

### Are maker fees always lower than taker fees?

Yes, OKX follows the industry-standard model where makers pay less than takers. The maker rate is 0.08% at the base tier and the taker rate is 0.10%. This ordering holds across all tiers, though the specific numbers change at higher volume bands.
