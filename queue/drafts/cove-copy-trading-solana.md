## How Cove copy trading works

Cove lets you automatically mirror another wallet's transactions. You pick a wallet that has been performing well, and the bot copies its trades in proportion to your allocated funds. If the wallet you are following buys a token, your bot buys it too. If it sells, your bot sells.

The mechanic is simple: you delegate the trading decision to someone else's judgement and let the bot handle execution. Cove supports both Solana and EVM chains, which means you can follow wallets trading on Ethereum, Base, BSC, and Solana from the same interface.

You create or import a wallet inside Cove, deposit SOL, pick a wallet to copy, and set the amount you want to allocate. The bot handles the rest.

## What outcomes to expect

Copy trading sounds straightforward, but the outcomes are less predictable than the pitch suggests. A wallet that was profitable last month may not be profitable this month. Markets rotate, strategies that worked in one regime fail in the next, and past performance is not a reliable indicator of future results.

Slippage is another factor. When you copy a trade, you execute after the wallet you are following. By the time your order goes through, the price may have moved. On thin tokens, that slippage can be severe — you might end up buying at a significantly higher price than the wallet you copied.

Your PnL will diverge from the wallet you are following, even if the strategy is working correctly. Entry price differences, fee accumulation, and timing gaps all push your results away from the original.

## Risks and what goes wrong

Funds are held by the bot, not in your own wallet. If Cove is compromised, your deposited SOL is at risk. This is the most significant structural risk of using any Telegram trading bot.

Beyond custody:

- A wallet that was profitable historically can turn unprofitable at any time
- Slippage is heavy on thin tokens and can turn a good trade into a loss
- Copy trading does not eliminate risk — it transfers the risk of a bad strategy from someone else to you
- New tokens, which are common copy-trading targets, are the highest-risk category in crypto

A large share of new tokens end in a rug pull. If the wallet you are following trades new tokens frequently, your copy trades will carry that same risk.

Copy trading is a tool for participation, not a shortcut to profit. The wallet you are following has no obligation to perform, and you have no control over its decisions once you enable copying.

It is also worth noting that copy trading works best when you diversify across multiple wallets rather than concentrating your funds on a single strategy. If one wallet turns unprofitable, others may offset the loss. This does not eliminate risk, but it reduces the impact of any single wallet's performance on your overall results.
