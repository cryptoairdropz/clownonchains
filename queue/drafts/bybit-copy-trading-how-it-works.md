Bybit copy trading lets you automatically mirror the trades of another trader on the platform. It sounds simple, but the mechanics, costs, and risks deserve a closer look before you commit funds. This article explains how the feature works, what it costs, and what can go wrong.

## How copy trading works on Bybit

When you copy trade on Bybit, you allocate a portion of your balance to follow a specific trader. Every time that trader opens or closes a position, the same action is executed in your account proportionally. If they open a position with 10% of their equity, your account opens a position with 10% of your allocated copy trading balance. The system replicates their trades automatically without requiring you to take any action. Bybit offers copy trading for both spot and futures markets. You can set a fixed allocation amount or let the system calculate based on the trader's risk settings. The trader you follow does not have access to your funds — they simply signal trades, and the platform executes them on your behalf. You retain full control over your account and can stop copying at any time. The copy trading interface shows each trader's historical performance, maximum drawdown, and current open positions so you can make an informed choice before committing your capital.

On top of that, copy traders may charge a profit-sharing fee, which is a percentage of the profits you generate from their signals. This fee varies by trader and is disclosed on their profile before you start copying. Some traders charge no profit share at all, while others take a significant cut of your gains. ## Risks and what goes wrong

Copy trading is not a guarantee of profit. A trader who was profitable last month can lose money this month. Past performance does not predict future results, and leveraged positions can amplify losses just as they amplify gains. The crypto market is volatile, and even experienced traders can experience prolonged drawdowns.

- The trader you follow may change strategy without warning
- High leverage can liquidate your copied positions quickly
- Slippage can cause your execution price to differ from the trader's
- You remain responsible for monitoring your own risk settings
- A trader's past returns may be the result of luck rather than skill
- Market conditions can shift rapidly, making previously profitable strategies unprofitable

Bybit's own documentation warns that copy trading is not a guarantee of profit. You should never allocate more than you can afford to lose. The platform's watch list explicitly states that copy trading carries risk and that users should understand what they are getting into before committing funds. Copy trading can be a useful tool for learning, but it is not a substitute for developing your own trading skills and risk management.

## Choosing a trader to follow

Look beyond the headline return percentage. Check the trader's maximum drawdown, their trading history length, and how many followers they have. A trader with a high return and 80% drawdown is far riskier than one with a moderate return and 10% drawdown. Also consider their trading frequency — a trader who makes 50 trades per day carries different risk than one who makes five trades per month. The referral code BYBIT313 gives new users access to fee discounts that make experimenting with smaller allocations more affordable. Start small, monitor for a few weeks, and scale up only if the trader's style matches your risk tolerance. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can I stop copying a trader at any time?

Yes. You can unfollow a trader at any time. Your open positions will be closed at market price, and your remaining balance returns to your available equity. There is no lock-in period or penalty for stopping.

### Does the trader I copy get access to my funds?

No. The trader only signals trades. Your funds stay in your own Bybit account and are never transferred to the trader. The platform simply mirrors their trading activity in your account.

### Is copy trading available on both spot and futures?

Yes, Bybit supports copy trading for both spot and futures markets. You choose which market to copy when you set up your allocation, and you can adjust or stop your settings at any time.
