Binance requires identity verification before users can deposit fiat, trade spot, or withdraw significant amounts. The process, known as KYC, involves submitting a government-issued ID and a selfie. Most applications complete within minutes, but certain errors cause delays or outright rejection. This guide covers what you need, how long it takes, and how to avoid the most common pitfalls.

## What documents Binance accepts

Binance accepts the following government-issued photo IDs: passport, national identity card, and driver's licence. The document must be valid, not expired, and show your full name, date of birth, and photo clearly. The name on the ID must match the name entered during registration exactly. If your account is registered under a nickname or a different spelling, verification will fail. In addition to the document, Binance requires a real-time selfie or a short video of you holding the document. This proves the ID belongs to you and is not a stolen photo. The system uses facial recognition technology to compare your selfie with the photo on the document.

## Step-by-step: completing KYC on Binance

- Log in to your Binance account and navigate to the verification section
- Select your country of residence, which determines which documents are accepted
- Choose the document type and upload clear photos of the front and back
- Complete the facial recognition step using your device's camera
- Submit and wait for the result

The entire process takes most users under ten minutes. The automated system checks document authenticity, facial match, and name consistency against government databases where available. Make sure you are in a well-lit room when taking the selfie, and remove any glasses or face coverings that might obscure your features.

## How long verification takes and why it stalls

Most KYC applications on Binance are reviewed within minutes to a few hours. During periods of high volume, such as a market rally when signups spike, the queue can extend to 24 to 48 hours. If your application is still pending after 48 hours, check that your photos meet the quality requirements before contacting support. Applications that are flagged for manual review take longer. This happens when the automated system detects an anomaly, such as a document from a country with high fraud rates or a name that appears on a watchlist. Manual review is not a rejection; it is an additional security step.

## The most common reasons for rejection

- Blurry or low-resolution photos where text on the document is unreadable
- Expired documents, even by a single day
- Name mismatch between the registration form and the ID document
- A selfie taken in poor lighting where facial features are obscured
- Using a screenshot of a document rather than a photo of the physical ID
- Attempting to verify with a document type not supported in your country

If your verification is rejected, read the rejection reason carefully before resubmitting. If the issue is photo quality, retake the image in bright, even lighting with the document lying flat on a dark surface. If the issue is a name mismatch, you may need to update your account information or provide additional proof such as a utility bill showing your name and address. Resubmitting immediately without fixing the root cause usually results in a second rejection.
