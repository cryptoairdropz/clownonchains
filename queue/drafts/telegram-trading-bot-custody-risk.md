Telegram trading bots promise speed and convenience — trade any token from any chain without leaving your chat app. But the feature that makes them appealing is also the one that puts your funds at risk: when you trade through a Telegram bot, your money sits in the bot's wallet, not yours.

## How a Telegram bot holds your funds

When you start a bot like Dawn, it generates a wallet for you or asks you to import an existing one. That wallet is controlled by the bot's infrastructure, not by a standalone application you own. You interact with it through Telegram commands, but the private keys are managed on the bot's side.

This is fundamentally different from using a hardware wallet or even a browser extension wallet where you hold the seed phrase. With a Telegram bot, you are trusting the bot's operators to secure your funds and to honour withdrawals. The convenience of trading from your chat app comes at the cost of custody.

## The custody risk in practice

If the bot is hacked, your funds are stolen and there is no recourse. If the bot's operators exit scam, your balance disappears. If the bot suffers a technical failure, you may be unable to withdraw for an extended period. These are not hypothetical scenarios — the history of crypto is littered with bots and services that lost user funds.

Dawn charges around 1% per trade, which is higher than a centralized exchange but in line with comparable Telegram bots. The fee is not the risk. The custody arrangement is.

## What makes Telegram bots attractive despite the risk

The value proposition is real, which is why these bots have large user bases.

- Speed: a bot can execute trades in seconds on tokens that are not listed on any exchange yet.
- Multichain access: bots like Dawn let you trade across several chains without manually bridging assets.
- No separate app: everything runs inside Telegram, which lowers the barrier to entry.
- Sniping: bots can participate in new token launches within seconds of liquidity being added.

These features solve genuine problems for active traders. The question is whether the custody trade-off is worth the speed.

## How to reduce your exposure

If you use a Telegram trading bot, treat it as a hot wallet for active trading, not a storage solution.

- Deposit only what you can afford to lose entirely.
- Withdraw profits regularly to a wallet you control.
- Do not store long-term holdings in a bot wallet.
- Enable every security feature the bot offers.
- Test with a small amount before depositing larger sums.
