Choosing between Binance and Bybit often comes down to fees, liquidity, and which market you trade most. Both exchanges offer a 20% referral discount to new users, but their base fee structures differ in ways that matter depending on whether you trade spot or futures. This comparison uses only published fee data to show where each platform wins.

## Spot trading fees on Binance vs Bybit

Binance charges a flat 0.10% on both maker and taker spot orders. Bybit, by contrast, uses a split structure: 0.02% for makers and 0.055% for takers. On a $1,000 spot trade, a Binance taker pays $1.00, while a Bybit taker pays $0.55 and a Bybit maker pays $0.20. For liquidity providers who place limit orders that rest on the book, Bybit is significantly cheaper. For market buyers who need immediate execution, the gap narrows but Bybit still holds a slight edge. A 20% referral discount on Binance brings the taker rate down to 0.08%, which closes the gap but does not fully close it. The maker fee on Bybit at 0.02% is five times lower than Binance's 0.10%, which is a significant advantage for high-volume limit order traders.

## Futures and derivatives fees

Bybit was built as a derivatives exchange first, and its futures fees reflect that focus. The platform is widely recognised for having the most competitive futures fees among major exchanges. Binance offers a full derivatives suite too, but its futures fee tiers are structured around trading volume thresholds that favour high-frequency professionals. For a beginner placing occasional leveraged trades, the difference in futures fees between the two platforms is small in dollar terms. For a high-volume trader executing hundreds of contracts daily, Bybit's lower base rates compound into meaningful savings over a month. Bybit also offers up to $30,000 in staged rewards for new users who meet deposit and volume requirements, which can further offset trading costs.

## How the referral discount changes the math

Both platforms offer a 20% fee discount through their referral programs. On Binance, the code RMCTNB5R applies this discount to spot trading for life. A 20% reduction on Bybit's 0.055% taker fee yields 0.044%. Bybit remains the cheaper option for spot takers even after both discounts are applied. For makers, the gap is even wider. The Binance discount is lifetime, while Bybit's promotional rates may have specific terms and conditions attached.

## Liquidity and slippage: the hidden cost

Fees are only part of the cost of trading. Slippage, the difference between the expected price and the filled price, can exceed the fee on large orders. Binance has the deepest liquidity of any exchange, which means large orders fill closer to the quoted price. Bybit's order books are deep on major pairs but can be thinner on mid-cap altcoins. For a trader moving $50,000 into a mid-cap token, Binance's lower slippage can easily save more than the fee difference. For a retail trader moving $500 in Bitcoin, the fee difference dominates and Bybit wins on cost. Binance also offers 350+ spot pairs, giving access to tokens that Bybit may not carry, which can be a deciding factor for altcoin traders. Other cost factors to consider include fiat deposit and withdrawal fees, network fees for crypto withdrawals, and the bonus structures each platform offers to new users. Bybit offers up to $30,000 in staged rewards, while Binance offers up to $100 in bonuses plus the lifetime 20% fee discount through the referral code RMCTNB5R.

No. Even with the 20% discount, Binance's effective taker fee of 0.08% remains above Bybit's 0.055% base rate. The discount narrows the gap but does not reverse it.
