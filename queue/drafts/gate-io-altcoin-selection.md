## How to filter the selection

Not all of Gate.io's 1,700+ pairs deserve your attention. Here is a framework for narrowing the field without relying on hype.

- Check daily volume: any pair with under $100,000 in 24-hour volume should be treated with extreme caution. Thin books mean your order moves the price against you.
- Verify the token's chain: some tokens exist on multiple chains. Make sure you are trading the version with real liquidity, not a bridge-wrapped copy with no market.
- Look at order book depth: a healthy book has bids and asks clustered tightly around the current price. Wide gaps between levels signal illiquidity.
- Research the team and tokenomics: anonymous teams, unaudited contracts, and tokens concentrated in a few wallets are red flags regardless of the exchange.

## The illiquidity trap

Gate.io's watch list explicitly warns that micro-cap tokens can be almost entirely illiquid. This is not a hypothetical risk. Tokens with market caps in the tens of thousands of dollars can have order books where a $500 sell order moves the price by double digits. In these conditions, the fee you pay is the least of your problems — the spread between bid and ask can consume a significant portion of your capital before any trade executes.

The 1,700+ pair count sounds like abundance, but much of it is a desert with scattered oases. Focus on the pairs with genuine volume and ignore the long tail unless you have a specific reason to be there.

## Risks of the deep selection

- Delisting risk: tokens on Gate.io can be removed from the platform, leaving holders unable to trade.
- Rug-pull exposure: micro-cap tokens with unaudited contracts can be drained by their creators.
- Fee drag: the 0.20% base rate means you need larger gains just to break even compared to lower-fee exchanges.
- Overwhelm: the sheer number of pairs can lead to impulsive trades on tokens you have not researched.

Treat Gate.io as a specialized tool for specific altcoins, not a default exchange for everything you buy.
