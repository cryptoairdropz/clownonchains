## What proof of reserves is

Proof of reserves is a method for exchanges to demonstrate they hold sufficient assets to cover customer balances. Bybit publishes periodic proofs that use cryptographic techniques to show the exchange controls enough cryptocurrency to match or exceed user deposits. The goal is to give users confidence that the exchange is not operating a fractional reserve. The process involves cryptographic verification that can be independently audited by anyone. The process typically involves the exchange publishing a Merkle tree of user balances and proving control of on-chain addresses that hold the assets. Users can verify their own balance is included in the tree without revealing their identity to other users. This allows each user to confirm that their funds are accounted for in the exchange's reserves without exposing their personal information.

## What proof of reserves does not prove

Proof of reserves has significant limitations. It shows that the exchange holds assets at a specific point in time, but it does not prove the exchange will hold those assets tomorrow. It also does not reveal the exchange's liabilities to other creditors, its off-chain obligations, or whether the assets are encumbered by loans. The audit is a snapshot, not a continuous guarantee.

- It is a snapshot, not a continuous guarantee
- It does not prove the exchange's total solvency, only that assets exceed user deposits
- It does not reveal off-chain liabilities or contingent obligations
- It does not prevent the exchange from misusing assets between audit dates
- It does not account for market risk on the assets held
- It does not reveal whether assets are lent out or otherwise encumbered

Bybit's watch list warns users to check the exchange's regulatory status before depositing serious money. Proof of reserves is a useful transparency tool, but it is not a substitute for regulatory oversight or insurance. Users should consider multiple factors when evaluating the safety of an exchange, not just proof of reserves.

## Risks and what goes wrong

Users often treat proof of reserves as a guarantee of safety. It is not. An exchange can pass a proof of reserves audit and still face insolvency due to bad loans, market losses, or fraud. The FTX collapse demonstrated that even exchanges with seemingly adequate reserves can fail catastrophically. Proof of reserves is a necessary but not sufficient condition for exchange safety.

- Proof of reserves does not protect against market risk on exchange-held assets
- It does not prevent the exchange from lending out user assets
- It does not guarantee the exchange will remain solvent between audit dates
- Users who rely solely on proof of reserves may ignore other risk factors
- The audit can be gamed if the exchange borrows assets temporarily
- Proof of reserves does not cover off-chain liabilities or contingent obligations

Bybit's proof of reserves is a positive step, but it should be one factor among many when deciding where to keep your crypto.
